The Case of Jane Doe

On 16 September 2026, a former Cornell student filed a civil lawsuit against the university. She alleged she was drugged and gang raped by seven students at the Chi Phi fraternity house in 2024 — and that Cornell failed to protect her and punish the alleged perpetrators.

The Night of October 19, 2024

What happened

A timeline drawn from Jane Doe's sworn statement to Cornell police, her Title IX testimony, and records reviewed by The New York Times.

8:30 PM

Jane Doe, a 20-year-old student, attends a sorority social event at Moonies Bar & Nightclub in Ithaca, New York. She alleges bar staff knew she was below the legal drinking age of 21 but still served her at least one drink.

By 11:00 PM

According to Doe, she had drunk approximately 10 drinks in three hours.

Late night

She walks about 1 mile from the bar to visit a male friend — Matthew Ingalls, a member of the school's Chi Phi chapter — at the fraternity house on 107 Edgemoor Lane.

Arrival

According to Doe, Ingalls took her inside, gave her another beer, and proposed a threesome with a second man. Her 2024 signed statement to police said she "agreed due to how attracted she was to him."

Before the activity

She consumed marijuana, rum, and ketamine with the men. She later noted it was her first time consuming ketamine, and that she was pressured to take it.

During

She reportedly developed bruising as a result of the sexual activity. She later engaged in further sexual activity with one of these men and a third man who entered the room.

1:42 AM

Some of the men exchange Snapchat messages in a group chat called "Chi Phi Actives".

Then

As four additional men entered the room, Doe was "horrified" and hid under the covers of the bed. According to her 2024 report, these men ingested ketamine and a majority left after making additional advances.

Throughout

At various points, she allegedly told some of them she was "uncomfortable" and "tired".

Later

As the majority of the men left, two of them removed her to another room. In her six-page sworn statement to Cornell police she said: "I had become extremely uncomfortable with how this night was turning out, so my thought was, if I do ketamine again, maybe I will pass out. My thought was that if I passed out and these men have sex with me, then they could have been charged with rape at this point."

At the hearing

At Cornell's Title IX hearing in May 2025, Doe alleged these two men were "just raping" her as she was "in and out of consciousness." She described it as her "worst" experience of the night.

Her account

In her longer initial statement to police, she said her eyes were "looking in different directions" and she became "completely numb to the situation."

Aftermath

Prosecutors did not seek additional records of the incident beyond Doe's six-page statement to campus police, and did not review the voluminous record of the internal probe.

The Days That Followed

The case takes a new turn

October 20

According to records reviewed by The New York Times, Jane Doe returned to her residence on the morning of October 20, 2024, after spending the night at Chi Phi.

October 21

Doe reportedly told her sorority "big sister" that people were calling her a "sl*t". Around the same period, she exchanged text messages with Jonathan Newell, one of the men she would later accuse.

October 22

She visited Cornell Health for a urinary tract infection and sought testing for sexually transmitted diseases. According to The Times, she told investigators she described the infection to the doctor as having occurred in a "traumatic way."

Three weeks later

She called Matthew Ingalls — the friend who invited her to the house — and told him: "...if I wanted to take this somewhere, what y'all did would be classified as gang rape... you already knew when I came in I was 10 shots down."
